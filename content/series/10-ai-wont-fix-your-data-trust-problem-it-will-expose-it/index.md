Your organisation decides to build an AI product. You have the data. You have the budget. You have a use case.

Then you start.

Half the expected data was never migrated from the old system. The data that did migrate still has the old tag names. Nobody flagged it - because Jose was fixing it manually for the Powerpoints that managers see.

One dataset gets copied in manually every Thursday. By a responsible, reliable employee who left 3 months ago. Nobody noticed because the only report that uses it - nobody checks anymore.

And the one you don't know about yet: your scoping consultation only included 3 of 5 senior leaders. The other 2 weren't invited because of a falling-out at a 2019 Thanksgiving party. Except one of them holds all the financial data. The only reason anyone gets it is because Jacob in accounting's girlfriend knows the data engineer, who drops it as a CSV when he gets a chance.

Some of you are thinking: this is a data governance problem. You're right.

But governance reviews have no budget, no sponsor, and no deadline anyone cares about. AI has all three.

You don't do AI work instead of governance work. You do the AI work to do the governance work.

The process of trying to create a system will expose the ways in which your organisation is not one.
