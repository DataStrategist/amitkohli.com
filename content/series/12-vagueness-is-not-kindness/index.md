At the AI Summit a couple of weeks ago, someone asked me (anonymously): "should we just be more tolerant rather than having a formal AI policy?"

I understood the instinct. Policies feel rigid. "Tolerant" sounds modern and humble.

But - tolerant of what, exactly?

Your staff are already using AI. Right now. Some brilliantly. Some in ways that will eventually embarrass your organisation.

And when something goes wrong - and something will - they're going to say: "nobody told me not to."

They're right.

There are two kinds of uncertainty. The kind your people can figure out - let them. The kind that needs someone with authority to actually decide - who is accountable when the output is wrong? What data can we share with external models? What can't we?

"Be tolerant" doesn't answer any of those questions. It just means nobody has to.

A policy isn't about distrust. It's about giving people something to stand on.

Vagueness is not kindness. Clarity is.
